Membrane
Potentials

Resting membrane potential, graded potentials, and the action potential — from ion gradients to neurotransmitter release.

−70 mV
Resting membrane potential (RMP) — the voltage difference across the cell membrane when the neuron is at rest. Present in all cells, not just neurons. Range: −70 to −90 mV; −70 mV is the standard textbook value.

Three contributors to RMP

Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase

Pump
  • Pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in
  • Net loss of cations → slight negative interior (≈ −5 mV contribution)
  • Also establishes the concentration gradients that drive the other two mechanisms

Leaky K⁺ channels

Always open
  • K⁺ is concentrated inside (by pump), so it leaks out down its gradient
  • Negative anions (proteins, phosphates) left behind
  • Largest contributor → pulls voltage toward ≈ −90 mV

Leaky Na⁺ channels

Always open
  • Na⁺ concentrated outside, so it leaks in
  • Cell is far more permeable to K⁺ than Na⁺
  • Partially counteracts K⁺ leak → raises voltage back from −90 to ≈ −70 mV

Nernst potential equation

Eion — equilibrium potential

Quantitative
Eion = (61.5 / z) × log([ion]out / [ion]in)
When to use When net ion movement = 0; concentration gradient force is exactly balanced by electrostatic force
EK⁺ result ≈ −90 mV  (K⁺: 5 mM outside / 150 mM inside)
ENa⁺ result ≈ +70 mV  (Na⁺: 140 mM outside / 10 mM inside)
Combined RMP Weighted by permeability (~90% K⁺, ~10% Na⁺) → ≈ −70 mV

Membrane permeability weighting

K⁺ 90%
Na⁺ 10%

High K⁺ permeability explains why RMP (−70 mV) sits closer to EK⁺ (−90 mV) than to ENa⁺ (+70 mV).

−55 mV
Threshold. Graded potentials move RMP (−70 mV) either toward or away from this value to control whether an action potential fires at the axon hillock.

EPSP vs IPSP

EPSP

Excitatory
  • Depolarizes the cell — inside becomes more positive
  • Moves RMP toward threshold: −70 → −55 mV
  • Example neurotransmitter: glutamate
  • Opens ligand-gated channels → Na⁺ / Ca²⁺ enter → interior becomes positive

IPSP

Inhibitory
  • Hyperpolarizes the cell — inside becomes more negative
  • Moves RMP away from threshold: −70 → −90 mV
  • Example neurotransmitter: GABA
  • Opens ligand-gated channels → Cl⁻ enters or K⁺ exits → interior more negative

Summation — how EPSPs accumulate to reach threshold

Temporal summation

Time
  • One presynaptic neuron fires repeatedly on one postsynaptic neuron
  • EPSPs arrive in quick succession and stack over time
  • Cumulative depolarization eventually reaches −55 mV threshold

Spatial summation

Space
  • Multiple presynaptic neurons fire simultaneously on one postsynaptic neuron
  • Their individual EPSPs add together at the soma
  • Combined depolarization reaches −55 mV threshold

Action potential waveform

Step-by-step sequence

① RMP Cell sits at −70 mV. Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels closed — activation gate shut, inactivation gate open.
② Threshold −55 Enough EPSPs depolarize the axon hillock. Activation gates of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open.
③ Rising phase Na⁺ floods in rapidly. Voltage rises from −55 → +30 mV (depolarization).
④ Peak +30 Inactivation gates of Na⁺ channels close. Voltage-gated K⁺ channels begin to open.
⑤ Repolarization K⁺ rushes out → voltage falls from +30 → −90 mV.
⑥ Hyperpolarization Voltage-gated K⁺ channels are slow to close → brief overshoot to ≈ −90 mV.
⑦ Return to RMP Na⁺/K⁺ ATPases + leaky channels restore −70 mV. All channels reset to resting state.

Na⁺ channel gate states

Voltage-gated Na⁺ channel — three states

Gates
At rest (−70 mV) Activation gate closed  ·  inactivation gate open  ·  Na⁺ cannot flow
Stimulated (−55 mV) Activation gate opens  ·  inactivation gate begins closing  ·  Na⁺ rushes in
Peak (+30 mV) Activation gate open  ·  inactivation gate fully closed  ·  Na⁺ entry halted

Axon terminal — Ca²⁺ and neurotransmitter release

Ca²⁺-triggered exocytosis

Output
  • +30 mV at terminal → voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → Ca²⁺ enters
  • Ca²⁺ binds SNARE proteins → synaptic vesicles fuse with plasma membrane
  • Neurotransmitters released into synaptic cleft via exocytosis
  • Repolarization (−90 mV) closes Ca²⁺ channels → release terminated

Refractory periods

Absolute refractory period

Spans from peak (+30 mV) until return to RMP. Inactivation gates are closed — no stimulus can trigger another action potential, no matter how strong. Sets a maximum firing frequency.

Relative refractory period

During hyperpolarization (≈ −90 mV). An action potential is possible but requires a stronger-than-normal stimulus to reach threshold from the more negative starting point.

Key terms quick reference

Depolarization Inside becomes more positive (e.g., −70 → +30 mV)
Repolarization Inside returns from positive back toward RMP (negative)
Hyperpolarization Inside becomes more negative than RMP (e.g., −70 → −90 mV)
Ligand-gated channel Opens when a neurotransmitter (ligand) binds — used in graded potentials at dendrites and soma
Voltage-gated channel Opens at a specific voltage: Na⁺ at −55 mV · K⁺ at +30 mV · Ca²⁺ at +30 mV
Presynaptic neuron The neuron sending the signal (upstream of the synapse)
Postsynaptic neuron The neuron receiving the signal (downstream of the synapse)
Axon hillock Trigger zone where action potentials are initiated; highest density of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels
