The ventricular system
Overview
Ventricles are fluid-filled cavities within the brain containing cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). There are four ventricles total.
CSF is made byChoroid plexus — pink, gummy tissue found in every ventricle
CSF is circulated byEpendymal cells (part of the choroid plexus)
Lateral ventricles (×2)
NumberTwo — one left, one right
PartsAnterior horn · Posterior horn · Inferior horn
SeparatorSeptum pellucidum — thin membrane between the two lateral ventricles
Choroid plexusPresent in each lateral ventricle; produces CSF
Drains viaInterventricular foramen (also called foramen of Monro) → 3rd ventricle
Third ventricle
Receives CSF fromBoth lateral ventricles via interventricular foramina
Choroid plexusPresent; produces CSF
Drains viaCerebral aqueduct (runs through the midbrain) → 4th ventricle
Fourth ventricle
Receives CSF fromThird ventricle via cerebral aqueduct
Choroid plexusPresent; produces CSF
CSF exits via (×2)Lateral apertures = foramina of Luschka (one each side)
CSF exits via (×1)Median aperture = foramen of Magendie (midline)
All apertures drain intoSubarachnoid space
Alternative routeCSF can also travel down the central canal of the spinal cord
CSF flow pathway
Lateral ventricles → Interventricular foramen → 3rd ventricle → Cerebral aqueduct → 4th ventricle

Choroid plexusCSF-producing tissue in every ventricle
Septum pellucidumThin membrane separating the two lateral ventricles
Interventricular foramenConnects lateral ventricles → 3rd ventricle (= foramen of Monro)
Cerebral aqueductConnects 3rd → 4th ventricle; passes through midbrain
Foramina of LuschkaLateral apertures of 4th ventricle (×2)
Foramen of MagendieMedian aperture of 4th ventricle (×1)
Subarachnoid spaceWhere CSF goes after leaving the 4th ventricle
