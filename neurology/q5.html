Resting Membrane Potential
Q1 Resting Potential Multiple choice
What is the standard textbook value for the resting membrane potential of a neuron?
Explanation
The resting membrane potential is −70 mV (range −70 to −90 mV). −55 mV is the threshold, +30 mV is the action potential peak, and −90 mV is the Nernst equilibrium potential for K⁺.
Q2 Resting Potential True / False
Resting membrane potential exists only in neurons, not in other cell types.
Explanation
False. Resting membrane potential exists in all cells, not just neurons. It is especially well-studied in neurons and muscle cells, but every cell with a plasma membrane maintains some resting voltage.
Q3 Resting Potential Multiple choice
The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump moves ions in which ratio?
Explanation
The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per cycle, using one ATP. This net loss of positive charges slightly hyperpolarizes the cell (≈ −5 mV contribution) and establishes concentration gradients for both ions.
Q4 Resting Potential Multiple choice
Which channel type is the largest contributor to bringing the resting membrane potential to approximately −90 mV (before Na⁺ leakage raises it back)?
Explanation
Leaky K⁺ channels are always open and allow K⁺ to leak out down its concentration gradient (K⁺ is concentrated inside by the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase). As K⁺ exits, it leaves negatively charged anions (proteins, phosphates) behind, pulling voltage toward ≈ −90 mV.
Q5 Resting Potential True / False
At rest, the cell membrane is more permeable to Na⁺ than to K⁺.
Explanation
False. At rest, the cell is significantly more permeable to K⁺ than Na⁺ (~90% K⁺, ~10% Na⁺). This is why the RMP (−70 mV) sits closer to the K⁺ equilibrium potential (−90 mV) than the Na⁺ equilibrium potential (+70 mV).
Q6 Resting Potential Multiple choice
What is the approximate concentration of K⁺ outside the cell (used in Nernst calculations)?
Explanation
K⁺ outside ≈ 5 mM; K⁺ inside ≈ 150 mM. The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase concentrates K⁺ inside, so extracellular K⁺ is much lower. (140 mM and 10 mM refer to Na⁺ outside and inside, respectively.)
Q7 Resting Potential Multiple choice
The anions (proteins and phosphates) left behind when K⁺ leaks out of the cell cannot exit because they are:
Explanation
These anions (proteins with many amino-acid negative charges, and phosphates) are too large and too negatively charged to exit through channels. They remain trapped intracellularly, making the inside progressively more negative as K⁺ leaves.
Q8 Resting Potential True / False
Leaky Na⁺ channels at rest cause Na⁺ to move into the cell, making the inside slightly more positive.
Explanation
True. Na⁺ is concentrated outside (140 mM) relative to inside (10 mM), so it moves in through leaky Na⁺ channels down its concentration gradient. This partially counteracts K⁺ leakage, raising RMP from ≈ −90 mV back to ≈ −70 mV.
Graded Potentials
Q9 Graded Potentials Multiple choice
What is the approximate threshold potential at which voltage-gated Na⁺ channels in the axon hillock open?
Explanation
The threshold potential is ≈ −55 mV. EPSPs must depolarize the axon hillock from RMP (−70 mV) to this voltage to trigger an action potential by opening voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.
Q10 Graded Potentials Multiple choice
Which neurotransmitter is classically associated with generating an EPSP in the central nervous system?
Explanation
Glutamate is the primary excitatory neurotransmitter in the CNS. It binds to ligand-gated channels, opening them to let Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ enter, depolarizing the postsynaptic cell toward threshold. GABA is the primary inhibitory transmitter.
Q11 Graded Potentials Multiple choice
GABA binding to its ligand-gated channel causes which ion movement to generate an IPSP?
Explanation
GABA opens ligand-gated channels that allow Cl⁻ to enter (adding negative charge) or K⁺ to exit (removing positive charge). Both effects make the inside more negative, hyperpolarizing the cell from −70 mV toward −90 mV — an IPSP.
Q12 Graded Potentials True / False
Temporal summation involves multiple presynaptic neurons firing simultaneously onto one postsynaptic neuron.
Explanation
False. That describes spatial summation. Temporal summation is when one presynaptic neuron fires repeatedly in quick succession, and those EPSPs stack over time to reach threshold.
Q13 Graded Potentials Multiple choice
An IPSP hyperpolarizes the postsynaptic cell. Which voltage does the cell reach during a typical IPSP?
Explanation
An IPSP moves the cell from RMP (−70 mV) to approximately −90 mV, making it harder to excite. The cell is now 35 mV away from threshold (−55 mV) instead of 15 mV.
Q14 Graded Potentials Multiple choice
Ligand-gated ion channels at the synapse open when:
Explanation
Ligand-gated channels open when a neurotransmitter (ligand) binds to the channel's receptor pocket — not in response to voltage. This is distinct from voltage-gated channels (Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺) which open at specific membrane voltages.
Q15 Graded Potentials True / False
If a graded potential (EPSP) does not reach threshold, an action potential will still fire (sub-threshold stimuli still produce APs).
Explanation
False. The action potential obeys an all-or-none principle. If the EPSP does not reach the threshold voltage (≈ −55 mV), voltage-gated Na⁺ channels at the axon hillock do not open and no action potential fires.
Action Potential
Q16 Action Potential Multiple choice
What is the anatomical "trigger zone" where action potentials are initiated in a neuron?
Explanation
The axon hillock is the trigger zone — the narrowing point between the cell body and the axon where voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are most concentrated. Once threshold is reached here, an action potential is initiated.
Q17 Action Potential Multiple choice
During the rising phase of an action potential, Na⁺ floods into the cell. At approximately what voltage does the inactivation gate of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels close, stopping Na⁺ influx?
Explanation
At +30 mV (the action potential peak), the inactivation gate of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels closes completely, preventing further Na⁺ entry. Simultaneously, this voltage opens voltage-gated K⁺ channels, beginning repolarization.
Q18 Action Potential Multiple choice
Repolarization of the action potential occurs primarily because:
Explanation
At +30 mV, voltage-gated K⁺ channels open and K⁺ exits the cell down its concentration gradient, carrying positive charge out and driving the membrane voltage from +30 mV back toward (and slightly past) RMP.
Q19 Action Potential True / False
During the absolute refractory period, a strong enough stimulus can still trigger a second action potential.
Explanation
False. During the absolute refractory period (peak to RMP), the inactivation gates of Na⁺ channels are physically closed — no stimulus, however strong, can open them. A second AP is impossible until the cell returns to RMP and channels reset.
Q20 Action Potential Multiple choice
Why does the membrane transiently hyperpolarize to ≈ −90 mV at the end of an action potential?
Explanation
Voltage-gated K⁺ channels are slow to close even after RMP is restored, so more K⁺ than usual exits, temporarily overshooting to ≈ −90 mV (hyperpolarization). The three resting mechanisms (ATPase + leaky channels) then return voltage to −70 mV.
Q21 Action Potential Multiple choice
What is the role of Ca²⁺ entering the axon terminal during an action potential?
Explanation
Ca²⁺ enters through voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels at +30 mV and binds SNARE proteins on both the synaptic vesicle and the plasma membrane. This links and fuses them, causing exocytosis of neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft.
Q22 Action Potential Multiple choice
During the relative refractory period, what is true about triggering another action potential?
Explanation
During the relative refractory period, the cell is hyperpolarized (≈ −90 mV). An AP is possible, but the stimulus must be stronger than normal — it must first bring the cell from −90 mV back to RMP, and then from RMP to threshold (−55 mV).
Q23 Action Potential True / False
The action potential propagates in both directions along the axon simultaneously.
Explanation
False. The action potential propagates unidirectionally from the axon hillock toward the axon terminal. The absolute refractory period behind the wave prevents the AP from traveling backward — channels just fired are inactivated and cannot be re-triggered immediately.
Q24 Action Potential Multiple choice
Which correctly lists the sequence of events in an action potential?
Explanation
The correct order is: threshold reached → Na⁺ enters (depolarization/rising phase, −55 → +30 mV) → K⁺ exits (repolarization, +30 → −90 mV with brief hyperpolarization) → leaky channels and ATPase restore RMP (−70 mV).
Nernst Equation
Q25 Nernst Equation Multiple choice
In the Nernst equation E = (61.5 / z) × log([ion]out / [ion]in), what does the variable z represent?
Explanation
In the Nernst equation, z = valence (charge) of the ion. For K⁺ and Na⁺, z = +1; for Ca²⁺, z = +2; for Cl⁻, z = −1. 61.5 is a constant derived from RT/F at body temperature.
Q26 Nernst Equation Multiple choice
The Nernst equilibrium potential for K⁺ (EK⁺) is approximately:
Explanation
EK⁺ ≈ −90 mV (K⁺: 5 mM outside / 150 mM inside). ENa⁺ ≈ +70 mV. The actual RMP (−70 mV) falls between these, weighted toward EK⁺ because the membrane is ~90% permeable to K⁺.
Q27 Nernst Equation Multiple choice
The Nernst potential is used when an ion has reached electrochemical equilibrium. What does this mean?
Explanation
Electrochemical equilibrium means the concentration gradient driving the ion one way is exactly balanced by the electrostatic gradient pulling it the other way. Net movement = 0. For K⁺, this occurs at −90 mV; ions still move in both directions, but at equal rates.
Channel Mechanics
Q28 Channel Mechanics Multiple choice
At resting membrane potential (−70 mV), the voltage-gated Na⁺ channel is in which state?
Explanation
At rest: activation gate (outside) = closed; inactivation gate (inside) = open. The channel is ready to be triggered but not conducting. At threshold: activation opens. At +30 mV: inactivation gate closes (absolute refractory). At return to RMP: reset to resting state.
Q29 Channel Mechanics True / False
Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in the axon terminal open at the same voltage (+30 mV) as voltage-gated K⁺ channels during an action potential.
Explanation
True. Both voltage-gated K⁺ channels and voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels (in the axon terminal) open at +30 mV. K⁺ exits to repolarize the cell; Ca²⁺ enters the terminal to trigger neurotransmitter release via SNARE-mediated exocytosis.
Q30 Channel Mechanics Multiple choice
Which of the following correctly describes the state of the voltage-gated Na⁺ channel at the PEAK (+30 mV) of the action potential?
Explanation
At peak (+30 mV): activation gate = open, inactivation gate = fully closed. Na⁺ cannot enter even though the activation gate is open — the inactivation gate physically blocks the channel pore. This is the hallmark of the absolute refractory period.
