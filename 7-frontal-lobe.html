Neuroscience · Unit 4

Frontal
Lobe

Anatomy, functional regions, output tracts, and clinical correlations — from voluntary motor control to language production and executive function.

Anatomy
Boundaries of the Frontal Lobe
Posterior boundary (from Parietal lobe)
Central sulcus — the primary anatomical boundary posterior to the frontal lobe. Immediately anterior to it lies the pre-central gyrus (primary motor cortex).
Inferior boundary (from Temporal lobe)
Lateral sulcus (Sylvian fissure) — runs inferiorly and separates the frontal lobe from the temporal lobe below it.
Area 4
Primary Motor Cortex
Pre-central gyrus · Brodmann Area 4

Location & core function

Execution
  • Just anterior to the central sulcus = the pre-central gyrus, Brodmann Area 4
  • Initiates and executes voluntary skeletal muscle movement
  • Receives input from motor association cortex, basal ganglia, and cerebellum (via thalamus)

Output tracts

Corticospinal tract (CST)
Cortex → spinal cord anterior gray horn → limb & trunk muscles (appendicular + axial). Controls fine voluntary movement of extremities.
Corticobulbar tract
Cortex → brainstem cranial nerve nuclei → head & neck muscles. Controls facial expression, speech, swallowing.

Cranial nerves supplied via corticobulbar

CN V — muscles of mastication CN VII — facial expression CN IX/X/XI — pharynx, uvula, soft palate, larynx, SCM, trapezius CN XII — tongue (hypoglossal)

Key rule: The size of a body region in the homunculus reflects the number of motor units and degree of fine motor control, not physical body size. Hands and face are disproportionately large.
Rx
Clinical correlation — Stroke & the homunculus

ACA (anterior cerebral artery) occlusion → medial cortex affected → lower extremity paralysis (foot, leg, hip regions are medial on the homunculus).

MCA (middle cerebral artery) occlusion → lateral cortex affected → upper extremity and face/neck paralysis (hand, face, tongue are lateral on the homunculus).

Area 6
Motor Association Cortex
Pre-motor + Supplementary Motor · Brodmann Area 6
Pre-motor cortex
Lateral portion of Area 6. Plans and sequences externally guided movements — responding to sensory cues from the environment.
Supplementary motor area (SMA)
Medial-superior portion of Area 6. Involved in internally generated and self-initiated movement sequences (movement from memory).

Functions

Planning of movement Sequencing of movement Execution of movement Fine motor of proximal muscles

Contribution to corticospinal tract

~15% of CST
  • Contributes approximately 15% of corticospinal tract fibers (primary motor cortex contributes ~85%)
  • Specifically innervates axial musculature (trunk) and proximal limb muscles (hip and shoulder girdles)

Key connections for planning & sequencing

Basal ganglia
Initiate wanted movements, suppress unwanted movements, and modulate overall motor output. Disruption → Parkinson's / Huntington's motor patterns.
Cerebellum
Receives proprioception + equilibrium from inner ear; sends motor corrections back up (via thalamus) to refine the motor plan in real time.
Area 8
Frontal Eye Fields (FEF)
Voluntary saccadic eye movements · Brodmann Area 8

Location & function

Saccades
  • Anterior to motor association cortex; considered part of the prefrontal cortex, Brodmann Area 8
  • Controls voluntary saccadic (rapid conjugate) eye movements — quickly shifting gaze to a new target
  • Each FEF drives gaze toward the contralateral side

Pathway — right FEF → contralateral (left) conjugate gaze

① Right FEF fires → projects to contralateral (left) PPRF (paramedian pontine reticular formation in the pons)
② Left PPRF → left CN VI nucleus (abducens)
③a Left CN VI → left lateral rectus → left eye abducts (moves left)
③b Left CN VI nucleus → via MLF (medial longitudinal fasciculus) → right CN III nucleus → right medial rectus → right eye adducts (also moves left)
✓ Result: Both eyes deviate conjugately to the LEFT (contralateral to the active FEF)
Rx
Clinical correlation — FEF lesion

Ipsilateral conjugate gaze deviation. Lesion of the right FEF → loss of the drive to look left → the unopposed left FEF drives gaze right → eyes deviate toward the right (same side as lesion).

Memory hook: after a FEF stroke, the patient's eyes "look at the lesion" (toward the damaged side).

Areas 8–14, 24, 25, 32, 45–47
Prefrontal Cortex
Prefrontal association area · executive function

Core functions

Personality & behavior Working memory Cognition / learning Reasoning & judgment Motor planning Behavioral inhibition

Key connections & what they mediate

Hippocampus
Working memory; encoding new long-term memories. Disruption → anterograde amnesia (cannot form new memories).
Limbic system (amygdala, hypothalamus)
Personality, behavior, and emotional regulation. PFC provides inhibitory control over raw emotional responses.
Ventral tegmental area (VTA)
Reward circuitry; dopaminergic input influences decision-making, motivation, and addictive behavior.
Posterior association area → basal ganglia
Integrates visual, auditory, and somatic stimuli to generate and refine complex motor plans.
Rx
Clinical correlation — Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD)

Personality / behavior changes (early): aggression, hostility, irritability, or apathy — first signs of PFC degeneration.

Working memory loss: difficulty forming new memories and impaired learning — typically later in the disease course.

Disinhibition: inappropriate hypersexual behavior, impulsive gambling — loss of the PFC's inhibitory "brake" on the limbic system.

Parkinsonian motor symptoms (late): damaged PFC → disrupted basal ganglia communication → motor deficits resembling Parkinson's disease.

Aphasia: temporal lobe co-involvement → impaired speech comprehension (Wernicke's-type).

Areas 44 / 45
Broca's Area
Inferior frontal gyrus · dominant hemisphere · speech production

Location & function

Speech production
  • Inferior frontal gyrus of the dominant hemisphere (left frontal lobe in right-handed individuals)
  • Brodmann Areas 44 & 45
  • Controls muscles of speech production — coordinates CN VII, IX/X/XI, and XII to produce articulate speech

Pathway — from comprehension to articulation

① Wernicke's area (temporal lobe, comprehension) processes language intent
② Signal travels via arcuate fasciculus → Broca's area (inferior frontal gyrus)
③ Broca's area generates motor program → sends via corticobulbar tract to cranial nerve nuclei
④a CN VII → orbicularis oris → mouth shaping
④b CN IX/X/XI (pharyngeal plexus) → soft palate, uvula, pharynx, larynx, vocal cords
④c CN XII (hypoglossal) → tongue articulation

Broca's vs Wernicke's aphasia

Broca's aphasia (expressive)
Cause: MCA lesion → Broca's area
Speech: non-fluent, effortful, grammatically impaired
Comprehension: intact
Patient understands but cannot speak fluently
Wernicke's aphasia (receptive)
Cause: MCA lesion → Wernicke's area (temporal)
Speech: fluent but meaningless ("word salad")
Comprehension: impaired
Patient speaks easily but cannot understand
Rx
Clinical correlation — Broca's aphasia

Cause: typically MCA (middle cerebral artery) lesion affecting the left inferior frontal gyrus.

Speech: non-fluent, grammatically incorrect, effortful — the motor program for speech is disrupted.

Comprehension: intact — Wernicke's area and the arcuate fasciculus are undamaged.

Also called expressive aphasia: the patient understands language but cannot produce it normally. Often associated with right-sided hemiparesis (MCA also supplies motor cortex).
