Neuron Structure
& Function

A comprehensive study guide covering neuronal anatomy, membrane potentials, axonal transport, and functional classification systems.

Parts of a neuron

Dendrites

Receptive zone
  • Branching extensions from the cell body that receive incoming signals
  • Primary site for receiving input from other neurons
  • Contain ligand-gated ion channels and G-protein-coupled receptors

Cell body (soma)

Integration zone
  • Contains the nucleus and all major organelles
  • Site of protein synthesis — neurotransmitters, enzymes, membrane proteins
  • Rough ER appears as Nissl bodies in neurons (unique name)
  • Also receives synaptic input; participates in graded potentials

Axon

Conducting zone
  • Axon hillock — where the cell body narrows into the axon; highest density of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels; site where action potentials are initiated
  • Long cable that conducts action potentials from soma to terminal
  • Contains microtubules that serve as tracks for axonal transport

Axon terminal (terminal bulb)

Output zone
  • Site of neurotransmitter release into the synapse
  • Site of neurotransmitter reuptake for recycling
  • Contains voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels and SNARE proteins for vesicle fusion

Dendrites & soma — graded potentials

EPSPs — excitatory postsynaptic potentials

Depolarizing
  • Excitatory neurotransmitter binds ligand-gated channel → channel opens → positive ions flow in
  • Inside of cell becomes more positive = depolarization
  • Goal: bring membrane toward threshold to fire an action potential

IPSPs — inhibitory postsynaptic potentials

Hyperpolarizing
  • Inhibitory neurotransmitter binds → channel opens → negative ions flow in
  • Inside of cell becomes more negative = hyperpolarization
  • Moves membrane further from threshold, inhibiting action potential firing

G-protein-coupled receptor pathway

  • Neurotransmitter → activates G-protein → second messengers → protein kinases → phosphorylate membrane proteins → ion flow → EPSP or IPSP
  • Slower than direct ligand-gated channels but yields the same net result

Axon — action potential conduction

Depolarizing wave

Na⁺
  • At threshold → voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open → Na⁺ rushes in → inside strongly positive
  • Positive charge flows down the axon toward the terminal

Repolarizing wave

K⁺
  • After depolarization → voltage-gated K⁺ channels open → K⁺ flows out → inside returns negative
  • Negative charge follows down, restoring resting membrane potential

Axon terminal — release & termination

Neurotransmitter release mechanism

Ca²⁺ dependent
  • Action potential arrives → voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → Ca²⁺ enters terminal
  • Ca²⁺ bridges v-SNAREs (synaptobrevin, synaptotagmin) on vesicle with t-SNAREs (syntaxin, SNAP-25) on membrane
  • Vesicle fuses with plasma membrane → neurotransmitters released into synaptic cleft

Neurotransmitter termination

  • Reuptake — transporter proteins pull NT back into the axon terminal for recycling
  • Enzymatic degradation — enzymes in the synapse break down the neurotransmitter
Rx
Clinical pearl — SSRIs

SSRIs (e.g., sertraline, fluoxetine, escitalopram) block serotonin reuptake transporters at the axon terminal → serotonin lingers in the synapse → prolonged receptor stimulation → clinical benefit in depression, anxiety, and OCD.

Motor proteins on microtubules

Soma → Terminal

Kinesin

Anterograde
  • Direction: cell body → axon terminal
  • Cargo: synaptic vesicles with neurotransmitters, membrane proteins, enzymes, mitochondria
Terminal → Soma

Dynein

Retrograde
  • Direction: axon terminal → cell body
  • Cargo: spent organelles for recycling, nerve growth factors to signal damage/repair to nucleus

Nerve growth factor (NGF) signaling loop

  • Damage at terminal → NGF released → dynein carries NGF retrogradely to nucleus
  • NGF stimulates gene expression → new mRNA → protein production
  • Newly synthesized proteins packaged into vesicles → kinesin ships them anterogradely to repair/grow the terminal
Rx
Clinical pearl — neurotropic viruses

Poliovirus, rabies, herpes simplex (HSV), and varicella-zoster (VZV) all hijack dynein (retrograde) to travel from nerve terminals up to the cell body/nucleus, where they replicate using the host's nuclear machinery.

In shingles (VZV reactivation), the virus then uses kinesin (anterograde) to travel back down to the skin → painful vesicular rash in a dermatomal distribution.

Structural classification

Multipolar

Most common
  • 3+ dendrites + 1 axon
  • Most common type in the CNS
  • Examples: pyramidal cells (motor cortex), Purkinje cells (cerebellum)
  • Multiple inputs needed — basal ganglia, cerebellum, sensory cortex all converge on motor cortex

Bipolar

Special senses
  • 1 dendrite + 1 axon
  • Found in retina, olfactory epithelium, vestibule/semicircular canals of inner ear
  • Generate graded potentials, not action potentials

Pseudo-unipolar

Sensory ganglia
  • Peripheral process — from periphery (e.g., skin) to cell body in ganglion
  • Central process — from cell body into spinal cord or brainstem
  • Cell bodies cluster outside CNS in ganglia
  • Key locations: dorsal root ganglia (spinal sensory) · trigeminal ganglion (CN V — face sensation, 3 divisions)

Functional classification

Sensory (afferent) neurons — toward CNS

Afferent
GSAGeneral somatic afferent — skin, skeletal muscle, joints
GVAGeneral visceral afferent — visceral organs (heart, lungs, GI, urogenital)
SSASpecial somatic afferent — vision, hearing
SVASpecial visceral afferent — smell, taste

Motor (efferent) neurons — away from CNS

Efferent
GSEGeneral somatic efferent — skeletal muscle
GVEGeneral visceral efferent — smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, glands (autonomic)
SVESpecial visceral efferent — muscles of head/neck from pharyngeal arches (CN V, VII, IX, X)

Interneurons — between sensory and motor

  • Relay and integration neurons; make up the majority of the CNS
  • Classic spinal reflex arc: skin stimulus → sensory neuron → interneuron → motor neuron → muscle contraction
  • Examples: nucleus gracilis & cuneatus (dorsal column pathway), thalamic relay nuclei
